Governors are never imposed upon the governed. This is a foundational principle of Presbyterian church polity:

> OPC FG III.2: "[Teaching and ruling elders] are acknowledged by the saints as those set over them in the Lord."

Put another way, "Ministers and elders are never imposed upon a congregation apart from their approval" (Dr. Alan Strange, [Commentary on the Form of Government](https://opc.org/os.html?article_id=836)).

If you join an Orthodox Presbyterian Church (or any Presbyterian church for that matter) with already called and elected ministers and elders, can you properly say that you have consented to the governors placed over you? In other words, is there any room for you to argue that the governors were imposed upon you because you were not involved in electing them?

The answer should be obvious, but I'm not sure it always is obvious. No. The fifth membership vow required of individuals who are joining as communing members in an Orthodox Presbyterian Church is: "Do you promise to participate faithfully in this church's worship and service, to **submit in the Lord to its government**, and to heed its discipline, even in case you should be found delinquent in doctrine or life?" (OPC DPW IV.B.2.5, emphasis mine). In taking this vow, you are consenting to be governed (spiritually speaking) by minister(s) and elder(s) that are already in place. You are promising to submit to the government of the church.[^removal]

If you ought not to be hasty in choosing new ministers and/or elders (OPC FG XXV.3, 1 Timothy 5:22: "Do not be hasty in the laying on of hands..."), then perhaps you ought not to be hasty in joining a particular church, and submitting yourself to its government. I am speaking, for example, of the occasion where you are moving to a new area and trying to find a church to transfer to. You should know the local leaders there well enough to vow submission to that church's government in the same way that you would know them to elect them to office.

This is particularly important if you are moving to an area in which there are very few Reformed churches and especially if you have chosen that area because there is a Reformed church there. Before moving, you should get to know that church, particularly its leaders, well.

These are some things to consider if you are currently looking for a church to join.

[^removal]: There are provisions in the Form of Government and Book of Discipline for the removal of ministers (requiring a majority vote of the congregation [OPC FG XXIV]) and elders (requiring a two-thirds majority [OPC FG XXVI.4.a.3]), but that is a different topic. And of course there are other ways that a ministerial relationship may be dissolved or an elder removed from office besides the congregation desiring it.
