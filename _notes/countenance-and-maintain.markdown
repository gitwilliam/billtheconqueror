Considering the phrase "countenance and maintain" in WLC 191, we ought to consider not only what it meant in 1646, and how did the meaning of it change in 1788, but also how does the church "imposing the oath" receive it? 

In 1646, it likely meant establishment. The 1788 changes to the confession must influence the way we read and understand it. 1788 certainly seems to be something less than 1646 when read in the context of the changes made to the confession. Then, according to Hodge (at least if you're an "honest [man]"),[^hodge] we should also consider how does the church "as a whole" (i.e. the OPC in my context) understand it today? What is the intention of the church imposing the oath? 

To argue that the OPC has understood WLC 191 "countenance and maintain" to mean establishment or "promotion of the true religion" seems like a stretch and as incorrect as to say that the OPC has understood WCF 21.5, "singing of psalms with grace in the heart" to require exclusive psalmody. 

For example, exclusive psalmody could be what 1646 intended (possibly), but that is not how the OPC, as a whole, has understood those words.
